================================================================================
COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS: DENSE, SPARSE, AND CROSS-ENCODER RETRIEVAL PARADIGMS
================================================================================

1. INTRODUCTION TO HYBRID RETRIEVAL
In modern Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipelines, relying solely on dense vector embeddings 
often leads to retrieval failures when handling domain-specific jargon, exact part numbers, acronyms, 
or rare product identifiers. Conversely, sparse keyword methods like BM25 fail when user queries 
employ synonyms, paraphrasing, or conceptual abstractions without exact token matches.

2. DENSE RETRIEVAL (BI-ENCODER)
Dense retrieval models (such as sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2) encode queries and passages 
into fixed-dimensional vector representations independently. Similarity is evaluated via dot product 
or cosine similarity. While computationally efficient (O(d) vector distance, accelerated by HNSW indexes), 
bi-encoders cannot capture cross-attention between specific query terms and passage tokens.

3. SPARSE RETRIEVAL (BM25 OKAPI)
BM25 relies on term frequency (TF) and inverse document frequency (IDF) with length normalization (k1=1.5, b=0.75).
It excels at exact lexical matching, technical identifiers, function names, and rare tokens, but suffers from 
vocabulary mismatch when synonyms or alternate phrasing are present.

4. CROSS-ENCODER RERANKING
Cross-encoders (e.g., cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2) process the query and candidate passage 
jointly through multi-head self-attention layers. This allows every query token to attend directly to every 
passage token, yielding significantly higher scoring accuracy and discrimination. Because cross-encoders are 
computationally expensive (O(N * L^2)), they are applied in a two-stage paradigm: first retrieve top-K candidates 
via hybrid dense+sparse retrieval, then rerank the top candidates to produce the final context window.
Empirical literature shows cross-encoder reranking consistently improves Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) and NDCG.
